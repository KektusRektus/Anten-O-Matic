Simulation
NEC-2 models conductors: wires, and plates as wire grids, and ignores dielectric blocks. openEMS models everything (plates and blocks must be aligned with the axes, wires are thin), and is much slower: evolve with small populations.

Antenna type
Antenna type
Passive elements are free bent wires (reflectors, directors) that the evolution shapes and places along with the arms. 0–4.
Draw the pattern you want on three planes in the Target tab.
Optimises the worst-case gain in a cone around the antenna axis (z axis). Point the axis where you want to receive from.
The antenna axis (z) points straight up, azimuth 0° = +x axis, elevation 90° = zenith. The guard band is the transition between the sector and the region the antenna should ignore. Symmetric types (bent dipole, 2 dipoles, quad) radiate equally forward and backward; azimuth directivity needs the asymmetric dipole, a single arm over ground, or passive elements.

NEC tuning
0 = formulas only, no tuning. Tunes lengths (and spacings for a Yagi) for gain, front/back ratio and SWR across the band.
Evolution
More bends and a bigger population find a better shape but take longer. The population is split into islands that evolve separately and exchange their best every 8 generations. An island that does not improve for N generations replaces half its shapes with new ones (0 = off). "Stop after" ends the evolution when the best shape has not improved for that many generations (0 = run to the end). "Max. similarity" guards against convergent evolution: when the best shapes of two islands stay more similar than the limit for a few generations, the weaker island drops everything similar and searches elsewhere (0 = off).
